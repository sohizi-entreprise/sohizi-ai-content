# Sohizi Lab — Landing Page Conversion Audit

**Reviewed:** September 28, 2026  
**Inputs:** Ten desktop screenshots, the current landing-page source, `product-description.md`, pricing content, and competitive/voice-of-customer research  
**Primary conversion:** Start creating free

## Executive diagnosis

The site has a credible visual identity: strong contrast, generous spacing, clear typography, a distinctive acid-green accent, and consistent cards and navigation. It looks like a serious software product.

The copy and proof do not yet meet that standard.

The page currently makes a familiar “all-in-one” argument, repeats benefits, targets several unrelated audiences, displays placeholder media as if it were proof, and includes an unfinished template feature grid. These issues do more than lower polish: they make visitors question whether the product itself is finished.

The most important change is to replace the **tool consolidation** story with a **production continuity** story:

> Sohizi is the project-aware workspace where a solo AI filmmaker keeps the script, scenes, references, generations, audio, and edit connected.

The page should then prove that promise by following one scene through the product.

## What already works

### Visual foundation

- The black, off-white, and acid-green system is memorable and consistent.
- The large editorial headings feel appropriate for filmmakers and creative software.
- Alternating light and dark sections create rhythm.
- The CTA is visually unmistakable.
- The capability diagram communicates that Sohizi is a hub rather than a single generator.
- The FAQ structure is easy to scan.

### Messaging foundation

- “One video. Eight tools. Zero memory.” is a strong problem frame.
- “The hard part was never the creativity. It's the commute between tools.” is memorable.
- The page recognizes exports, re-uploads, repeated prompts, and context loss.
- “An AI that remembers” points toward the right differentiator.
- “Start creating free” is consistent across the page.

These assets should be refined, not discarded.

## Priority findings

### P0 — Fix before driving meaningful traffic

#### 1. The hero leads with a commodity position

**Current:** “Make your whole AI video in one tool. Not eight.”

The line is clear but strategically weak. LTX, Runway, Firefly, Magnific, Katalist, Canva, and others can all claim broad end-to-end creation. Visitors who already know those products have no reason to believe Sohizi is meaningfully different.

**Change:** Lead with the project-aware continuity benefit and name solo AI filmmakers. Use the integrated toolset in the subheadline as the mechanism.

**Recommended direction:** “Your next shot should remember the film you're making.”

#### 2. The hero contains no product proof

The largest visual area is decorative: grid, particles, glowing arc, and oversized wordmark. None of it helps the visitor understand what Sohizi does or believe the core promise.

**Change:** Keep the visual atmosphere, but use the lower half of the hero for a real product composition. Show one project with:

- A script scene
- Character or location references
- The corresponding storyboard frame
- Generated takes for the shot
- The same shot on the timeline
- The assistant referencing those artifacts

The same scene must be recognizable across panels. That single visual can prove “the project stays connected” faster than a feature list.

#### 3. The showcase makes a proof claim while displaying placeholders

**Current:** “Every video below was written, generated, storyboarded, and edited inside a single Sohizi project.”

The visible cards are empty image placeholders. This creates a direct credibility failure: the copy asks visitors to trust evidence that is visibly absent.

**Change:** Until real work is available, remove the showcase claim and grid. Replace it with a **product proof walkthrough** using real UI and a clearly labeled sample project. When real output is ready, restore a smaller, curated showcase with documented provenance.

#### 4. The feature section is unfinished template content

The screenshots show claims such as “Built for developers,” “100% Uptime guarantee,” password sharing, “we will convince you to like us,” and an apology for running out of copy. This is the highest-risk section on the page.

It is unrelated to the product, includes questionable or false claims, refers to “EveryAI,” and can make a serious buyer abandon the site immediately.

**Change:** Remove this section completely. Do not rewrite it as eight generic feature cards. Replace it with three or four production outcomes supported by real UI.

#### 5. The how-it-works video does not prove the product workflow

The embedded “Hardest Truth” lyric video may show output, but it does not demonstrate that Sohizi created it or how the connected workflow works. The section title “Check out how it works” sets an expectation for a product demonstration.

**Change:** Replace it with a 45–75 second silent-friendly screen recording following one scene through Sohizi. Include captions so the proof works without audio.

### P1 — High-impact conversion improvements

#### 6. The page presents the problem before establishing product relevance

After the hero, the page immediately shows a large comparison table. The visitor has not yet seen the product, understood the target user, or gained confidence that Sohizi can deliver the promised alternative.

**Change:** Move product proof directly after the hero. Follow with the problem and a shorter before/after comparison after the mechanism is clear.

#### 7. “All in one tool” weakens the most distinctive section

The capability-hub visual is useful, but its headline turns it into a generic feature bundle. The rotating feature name also makes the promise feel like “we have many tools” rather than “the tools share project context.”

**Change:** Reframe the section as **“One project for the whole production.”** Explain that the connection between the tools—not their mere presence—is the product.

#### 8. Benefits repeat and overreach

“Consistency without babysitting every prompt” appears twice. “One subscription, not a stack” implies complete replacement of other products, which may not be true for specialists. “Your idea stays warm” is evocative but too abstract to carry a large card.

**Change:** Use four distinct outcomes:

1. The production has one home.
2. Approved references stay with the shot.
3. The assistant has the project in front of it.
4. The edit uses the assets already in the project.

Add subscription simplification as supporting copy, not a primary promise.

#### 9. The audience section is too broad for the current product

The page speaks to AI filmmakers, short-form creators, marketers, brand teams, agencies, and educators. The product description says the alpha focuses on individual creators and does not include team collaboration.

**Change:** Make solo AI filmmakers the primary audience. Mention music-video makers, brand storytellers, and YouTube creators as project types—not separate market segments. Remove agencies and teams until collaboration supports their needs.

#### 10. The comparison table contains claims that need qualification

Rows such as “style holds from first scene to final cut” and “one subscription for the full workflow” sound guaranteed. Model output can still vary, and some creators will keep specialized tools.

**Change:** Compare observable workflow behavior:

- References remain in the project.
- The assistant can use project files.
- Generated media is available to the storyboard and editor.
- The script, shots, and timeline can be viewed as one production.

Avoid guaranteeing output consistency.

#### 11. The CTA lacks a nearby risk reducer

The bright CTA is prominent, but the hero does not explain what “free” means.

**Change:** Add a short line below the primary CTA:

> 500 free credits. No card required.

Publish this only after confirming it matches the current sign-up and billing experience.

### P2 — Polish and optimization

#### 12. Navigation does not expose the differentiator

“Showcase” and “How it works” are reasonable, but the page lacks a path to the project-aware assistant or workflow.

**Change:** Use: Product, How it works, Pricing, FAQ. If the demo remains a major proof block, “See the workflow” is stronger than “Showcase.”

#### 13. Some section headings describe page mechanics rather than customer value

Examples: “Check out how it works,” “Packed with multiple features,” and “Frequently Asked Questions.”

**Change:** Use headings that advance the argument:

- “Watch one scene move through the whole production.”
- “Every part of the film stays connected.”
- “What creators ask before they start.”

#### 14. Repeated sticky navigation reduces visual focus in screenshots

The navigation remains visible throughout and changes contrast correctly, but its persistent brightness can compete with section content, particularly on light backgrounds.

**Change:** Keep sticky behavior, reduce the CTA glow after scrolling, and ensure the logo/nav contrast transition does not produce dark text over a dark transitional background.

#### 15. Footer CTA language is inconsistent

The primary CTA says “Start creating free,” while the footer says “Get started.”

**Change:** Use the same primary CTA label everywhere.

## Recommended page architecture

### 1. Navigation

- Logo
- Product
- How it works
- Pricing
- FAQ
- Sign in
- Start creating free

### 2. Hero: establish the category and promise

Purpose: Tell the right visitor what changes and why Sohizi is different.

Required elements:

- Audience/category eyebrow
- Continuity-led headline
- Concrete subheadline
- Primary CTA and free-credit support line
- Real product composition using one consistent scene

### 3. Product proof: show one scene staying connected

Purpose: Prove the mechanism before explaining it at length.

Use a short loop or stepped UI sequence:

1. Select a line in the script.
2. Ask the assistant to break it into a shot.
3. Show character and location references already present.
4. Generate storyboard options.
5. Approve a frame and generate the video.
6. Place the clip on the timeline with voice or music.

Caption: “The script, references, shot, takes, and edit stay in the same project.”

### 4. Problem: the production forgets itself between tools

Purpose: Make the experienced creator feel understood.

Focus on:

- Re-explaining characters and style
- Losing the relationship between a reference and a take
- Tracking approved versions in folders
- Paying to regenerate from incomplete context
- Editing without the script and shot plan nearby

### 5. Solution: one project for the whole production

Purpose: Introduce the workspace model.

Use the current capability hub, but change its meaning: every tool connects to the project. The center should represent the project/context layer rather than only the Sohizi logo.

### 6. Differentiator: an assistant that has read the project

Purpose: Explain the durable advantage over a bundle of tools.

Show examples of questions or actions that depend on project context. Keep the examples within current product behavior.

### 7. Workflow: script to final cut

Purpose: Reduce perceived complexity.

Four steps:

1. Write
2. Plan
3. Generate
4. Edit

Each step should refer to the artifacts carried into the next step.

### 8. Outcomes: what changes when the project stays together

Purpose: Translate features into value.

Use four cards maximum. Pair each outcome with a real UI crop.

### 9. Comparison: prompt-by-prompt versus project-aware

Purpose: Make the alternative explicit after the visitor understands the product.

Use four behavior-level rows rather than six broad claims.

### 10. Audience fit

Purpose: Help the visitor self-qualify.

Speak to solo AI filmmakers making narrative shorts, cinematic ads, music videos, and story-led YouTube work. State that the current product is for individual creators.

### 11. FAQ

Purpose: Handle objections.

Cover current scope, existing tools, import/export, consistency, ownership, models, credits, and learning curve. Verify legal and billing answers before publishing.

### 12. Final CTA

Purpose: Restate the transformation and reduce risk.

Use the same CTA and free-credit support line as the hero.

## Product proof plan

Customer testimonials and performance metrics are not currently available. The page should use product evidence instead.

### Primary demo asset

Create one sample project called **The Last Signal** or another clearly fictional title. It should contain:

- A short script with one visually distinctive protagonist
- Character reference sheet
- Location reference
- Storyboard with at least three shots
- Multiple takes for one shot
- Voice or music asset
- A timeline containing the approved clips

The page should repeatedly use the same scene so the visitor can visually verify continuity.

### Hero visual

A composed product screenshot showing script, references, storyboard, assistant, and timeline. Do not show every panel at full detail; highlight the shared scene and use connecting labels.

### Workflow demo

A 45–75 second screen recording with on-screen captions:

1. “The scene starts in the script.”
2. “The assistant uses the characters and references already in the project.”
3. “Storyboard options stay attached to the shot.”
4. “The approved frame becomes the starting point for video.”
5. “The clip is already available in the edit.”

### Outcome evidence

Use UI crops rather than icons:

- Scene with related assets
- Character reference beside a generated take
- Assistant response with cited project files
- Timeline populated from project media

### Future social proof

When real users exist, collect:

- Project type and length
- Previous tool stack
- Where context or assets were lost before Sohizi
- A specific workflow improvement
- Permission to show the creator, finished video, and product project

Do not collect generic “I love it” quotes.

## Conversion and measurement plan

### Primary funnel

1. Landing-page visit
2. Primary CTA click
3. Sign-up started
4. Sign-up completed
5. First project created
6. First project artifact created
7. First generation completed
8. Return within seven days

The landing page should be judged primarily on qualified sign-ups and first-project creation, not CTA click-through alone.

### Recommended events

- `landing_view`
- `hero_cta_click`
- `nav_cta_click`
- `workflow_demo_start`
- `workflow_demo_complete`
- `pricing_view`
- `faq_expand` with question identifier
- `final_cta_click`
- `signup_started`
- `signup_completed`
- `first_project_created`

### First experiments

Run only after placeholders and template copy are removed.

1. **Hero promise**  
   A: “Your next shot should remember the film you're making.”  
   B: “Keep your whole AI film in one connected project.”

2. **Proof placement**  
   A: Product composition inside the hero  
   B: Product composition immediately below the hero

3. **CTA support line**  
   A: “500 free credits. No card required.”  
   B: “Create your first project free. No card required.”

4. **Differentiator frame**  
   A: “An assistant that has read the project.”  
   B: “AI that works from more than the latest prompt.”

### Guardrails

- Segment results by traffic source.
- Do not optimize solely for sign-ups if activation falls.
- Let each test run long enough to capture normal weekly variation.
- Change one major message at a time.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Delete the template feature section and false claims.
2. Replace the placeholder showcase with real product proof.
3. Rewrite the hero around project-aware continuity.
4. Add the free-credit/no-card support line after verification.
5. Reframe the capability hub around the connected project.
6. Remove duplicated and overreaching benefits.
7. Narrow the audience to solo AI filmmakers.
8. Replace the embedded output video with a workflow demo.
9. Rewrite comparison and FAQ claims to match current behavior.
10. Add conversion and activation analytics.
